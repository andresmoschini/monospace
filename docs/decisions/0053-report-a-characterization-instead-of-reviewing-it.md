# Report a characterization instead of reviewing it

## Context and Problem Statement

[ADR-0045](0045-pin-every-arrow-arrangement-as-a-reviewed-snapshot.md) pins 1,856 arrow
arrangements, 20,424 lines across eight files, and it was right about why: what a snapshot buys is
not correctness but a diff, so a change that moves a picture has to be looked at instead of passing
silently.

It pinned them as a **reviewed** snapshot — the claim that the 1,856 were read once against the
model, which that record itself says "only a person can keep", alongside "a reviewed-once artifact
decays" and "a file of 1,856 pictures is not something anyone reads end to end". The word turns an
instrument of visibility into an obligation of approval: every change to the ranking asks for a
reading nobody can perform, so the honest answer is to avoid changing the ranking.

## Decision Drivers

- The maintainer's question is not "did anything change?", which the test already answers, but "did
  it change the way I wanted?".
- A claim in a durable document that nobody can keep is worse than no claim: it is believed by
  whoever reads it next, and here it was believed as a reason not to change the code.

## Decision Outcome

Chosen option: **keep the sweep whole and drop the claim of review; require a report instead** —
because the sweep is what makes a change visible, and the report is what makes it legible.

Each time a characterization moves, what is produced is: **how many cases moved, in which families
of arrangement, and three representative examples with their before and after.** That report is what
is read, and the file is not claimed to be. The constitution's Testing section also splits the two
kinds apart: a contract test pins a decision, so moving one is a decision and belongs on the sheet;
a characterization records what the code does over a range too wide to assert by hand, and moving
one is the consequence of a decision taken elsewhere.

### Consequences

- Good, because changing the ranking stops being an act that requires re-certifying 1,856 pictures.
- Good, because the two kinds of snapshot become distinguishable at a glance, which is what a
  reviewer needs in order to know whether a diff is a decision or a derivation.
- Bad, because the report is produced by whoever accepts the diff and nothing in the gate checks it
  was produced, or that its three examples are representative.
- Neutral, because not one picture is removed, sampled or regenerated by this record.

## Reversibility

Cheap: it retires a sentence and adds a habit. What it does not undo is the sweep's real cost —
eight files and 20,424 lines move on any change to the ranking, and that is paid by the test, which
is where it belongs and where a reader meets it.

## Confidence

Medium-high (75%). What would change it: a report that turns out to be as unreadable as the file,
which would mean the families are the wrong unit to count by. What would prove it wrong: a defect
landing through an accepted diff whose report looked fine.
